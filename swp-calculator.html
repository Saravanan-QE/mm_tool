What is a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) lets you withdraw a fixed amount every month from your mutual fund investment. Instead of redeeming your entire corpus at once, SWP provides a steady stream of income while the remaining investment continues to earn returns.

SWP is popular among retirees and investors seeking regular income from their mutual fund corpus. It is more tax-efficient than Fixed Deposit interest or dividend payouts because only the capital gains portion of each withdrawal is taxed.

How the Calculation Works

The calculator simulates month-by-month withdrawals from your corpus:

Each month: New Balance = Old Balance x (1 + r/12) - Withdrawal

  • Old Balance = corpus at the start of the month
  • r = expected annual return (e.g. 0.10 for 10%)
  • Withdrawal = fixed monthly amount you take out

Example: ₹25,00,000 corpus at 10% p.a. with ₹15,000/month withdrawal for 15 years: you withdraw ₹27,00,000 total and still have a remaining corpus of approximately ₹29,69,000.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SWP in mutual funds?
A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) lets you withdraw a fixed amount every month from your mutual fund investment. It provides regular income while the remaining corpus stays invested and continues earning returns.
SWP vs FD interest — which is better?
SWP from equity funds offers potentially higher returns (10-12% vs 6-7% FD) and better tax efficiency. FD interest is fully taxable at your slab rate, while only the capital gains portion of SWP withdrawals is taxed.
Is SWP taxable in India?
Only the capital gains portion of each SWP withdrawal is taxed, not the full withdrawal amount. For equity funds held over 1 year, LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh is taxed at 12.5%. Debt fund gains are taxed at slab rate.
What is the minimum investment for SWP?
Most mutual funds require a minimum of ₹25,000 to start SWP. Some funds allow SWP with as low as ₹10,000. The minimum withdrawal per instalment is typically ₹500 to ₹1,000.
Can I change the SWP withdrawal amount?
Yes, most mutual funds allow you to modify your SWP amount, frequency, or stop the SWP at any time without penalty. Changes typically take effect from the next scheduled withdrawal date.
SWP vs dividend payout — which should I choose?
SWP is more tax-efficient than dividend payout. Dividends are taxed at your full slab rate, while SWP withdrawals are taxed only on the capital gains component. SWP also gives predictable, regular income.
