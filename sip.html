What Is an SIP?

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is a way to invest a fixed amount regularly — usually monthly — into a mutual fund of your choice. SIPs let you build wealth gradually while smoothing out market timing risk through rupee-cost averaging.

Each contribution buys more units when prices fall and fewer when they rise, so your average buy price tracks the market over time. Combine that with the long-term compounding effect of equity markets, and a disciplined SIP is one of the most reliable wealth-creation tools available to Indian retail investors.

  • Low barrier to entry — most fund houses allow SIPs starting at ₹500/month.
  • Hands-free — auto-debits ensure you never miss a contribution.
  • Goal-friendly — align SIP horizon with life goals like home, retirement, or child's education.

Ready to go further? If you can raise your investment as your income grows, a step-up SIP can add lakhs to your final corpus versus a flat SIP. And if you want your investment to double as a tax break, routing it through ELSS funds claims a Section 80C deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakh a year.

How Is the SIP Maturity Calculated?

This calculator uses the standard future value of an annuity formula with monthly compounding:

FV = P × [((1 + r)n − 1) / r] × (1 + r)

  • FV = future value (maturity amount)
  • P = monthly investment
  • r = monthly return rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = total number of months (tenure × 12)

The profit is simply FV minus your total invested (P × n). Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks; results are estimates based on a constant return.

A note on why calculators disagree. We convert your annual rate to a monthly one as rate ÷ 12 — so 12% a year is treated as 1% a month. Some calculators instead use the effective conversion, (1 + 12%)1/12 − 1, which works out to about 0.949% a month and produces a lower figure. Neither is wrong; they answer slightly different questions. On ₹10,000 a month at 12% for 10 years the two methods give ₹23.23 lakh and ₹22.40 lakh respectively. Both are estimates — your actual returns will depend on the fund.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is SIP return calculated?
SIP returns use the formula FV = P × ((1 + r)n − 1) / r × (1 + r), where P is the monthly investment, r is the monthly return rate, and n is the number of months.
Why does another website show a different SIP maturity amount?
Because calculators split on how they turn an annual rate into a monthly one. We use rate ÷ 12, so 12% a year is 1% a month — the same convention Angel One uses. Groww and HDFC Mutual Fund use the effective conversion, (1 + annual)^(1/12) − 1, which is about 0.949% a month and gives a slightly lower figure. On ₹10,000/month at 12% for 10 years that is ₹23.23 lakh versus ₹22.40 lakh. Both are valid estimates of an uncertain future return.
What is the minimum amount to start an SIP?
Most mutual funds allow SIPs starting at ₹500–₹1,000 per month. Some funds permit weekly or fortnightly SIPs as well.
How to start an SIP in India for 2025?
Complete your KYC, pick a mutual fund scheme that matches your goal, set your monthly amount, and start via a platform (Groww, Zerodha Coin, Kuvera) or directly with the AMC.
Are SIP returns taxable in FY 2026-27?
Equity SIPs: LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh taxed at 12.5%; STCG at 20%. Debt SIPs: gains taxed at your income slab. Use our Income Tax Calculator for projection.
How does step-up SIP differ from a regular SIP?
A step-up SIP increases your contribution annually (commonly by 5–20%). Try our Step-Up SIP Calculator to see the impact on corpus.
How long should I run an SIP for good returns?
Equity SIPs benefit most from 7–10+ year horizons because compounding really kicks in. Short tenures expose you to market timing risk.
