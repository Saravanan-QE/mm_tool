Our editorial standards

Every calculator on MoneyMindTool follows the same process before it goes live and each time the law changes:

  • Sourced from the primary authority. Rates, slabs, limits and formulas come from official Indian government sources (listed below), never from second-hand summaries.
  • Formula-verified. Each calculation is checked against the published formula and cross-checked with worked examples. Where two conventions exist (for example, how an annual return is converted to monthly for SIPs), we state which one we use and why.
  • Dated and re-reviewed. Every page shows when it was last updated. When a rate, slab or rule changes, we revise the tool and the supporting content, and refresh the date.
  • Transparent about limits. Our tools give estimates based on your inputs and standard formulas. They do not replace personalised advice from a qualified professional — and we say so in our disclaimer.

How we build and test a calculator

  1. Identify the governing rule and the exact formula from the source authority.
  2. Implement it, then validate against hand-worked examples and, where available, the authority's own illustrations.
  3. Write plain-English guidance, FAQs and worked examples alongside the tool.
  4. Re-test on every rate or rule change and update the "last updated" date.

Official sources we rely on

Our numbers trace back to primary Indian government and regulatory sources:

  • Income Tax Department of India (CBDT) — tax slabs, deductions, TDS and capital-gains rules.
  • Reserve Bank of India (RBI) — repo rate, lending benchmarks and banking guidelines.
  • Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) — EPF and pension rules.
  • Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) — mutual fund and market regulations.
  • National Savings Institute — PPF, SSY and small-savings interest rates.
  • Goods and Services Tax Portal — GST rates, slabs and late-fee rules.
