Income-Tax Act 2025: Simplified Tax Law Effective April 2026 – Key Changes Explained

The new Income-Tax Act 2025 (enacted August 2025) replaces the 1961 Act with a simpler, modern framework. It reduces sections from over 800 to 536, uses plain language, removes redundancies, and introduces digital-first processes to cut litigation and boost compliance. Most provisions retain existing tax rates and rules but reorganize them logically. The Act applies from FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28). Use our Income Tax Calculator to see how it affects your planning.

Major Highlights of the New Act

  • Simplified Structure: 23 chapters, 16 schedules; easier navigation with unified "Tax Year" concept replacing Assessment/Previous Year.
  • Digital & Faceless Processes: Expanded faceless assessments, faster refunds, and prior notice for enforcement actions.
  • Key Clarifications: Standard deduction for house property, full commuted pension deduction, refined "business connection" and "associated enterprise" definitions.
  • No Major Rate Changes: Existing slabs, deductions (80C, 80D, etc.), and capital gains rules continue; focus is on clarity and ease.
  • Virtual Assets: Clearer taxation for crypto/NFTs as capital assets.

Benefits for Taxpayers

  • Reduced ambiguity leads to fewer disputes and penalties.
  • Easier self-filing with plain English provisions.
  • Better protection against arbitrary actions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the new Income-tax Act 2025 change tax rates?
No — it retains existing tax rates, slabs and deductions like 80C and 80D. The focus is on restructuring the law for clarity, not changing how much tax you pay.
How many sections does the new Act have compared to the old one?
It reduces the number of sections from over 800 to 536, organized into 23 chapters and 16 schedules.
From when does the new Act apply?
From FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28).
What is the "Tax Year" concept?
A unified replacement for the earlier "Assessment Year" and "Previous Year" terminology, meant to simplify which year's income and filing rules apply.
Does the Act address cryptocurrency and NFTs?
Yes — it provides clearer taxation treatment for virtual digital assets like crypto and NFTs as capital assets.
