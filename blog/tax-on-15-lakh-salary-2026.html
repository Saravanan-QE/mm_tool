How Much Tax Will I Pay on a ₹15 Lakh Salary? (FY 2025-26)

Short answer: ₹97,500 under the New Regime, if ₹15 lakh is your full gross salary with no other income. Under the Old Regime, it depends heavily on how many deductions you actually claim — anywhere from ₹1,40,400 to ₹2,57,400. Here's the exact math, using the same FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) slabs and standard deduction values as our Income Tax Calculator, so the numbers below match what the tool itself will show you.

New Regime — the only number that matters here

The New Regime doesn't reward extra deductions (beyond the standard deduction and employer NPS), so there's exactly one answer regardless of your investments or rent:

  • Gross salary: ₹15,00,000
  • Standard deduction: −₹75,000
  • Taxable income: ₹14,25,000

Applying the FY 2025-26 New Regime slabs (0% to ₹4L, 5% from ₹4–8L, 10% from ₹8–12L, 15% from ₹12–16L):

  • ₹0–4L: 0% → ₹0
  • ₹4–8L: 5% → ₹20,000
  • ₹8–12L: 10% → ₹40,000
  • ₹12–14.25L: 15% → ₹33,750

Base tax: ₹93,750. Add 4% cess: ₹3,750. Total: ₹97,500 — about ₹8,125/month.

Old Regime — three realistic scenarios

The Old Regime's standard deduction is ₹50,000 (not ₹75,000), so taxable income starts at ₹14,50,000 before any further deductions.

ScenarioTaxable IncomeTax Payable
No deductions beyond standard deduction₹14,50,000₹2,57,400
+ Section 80C (₹1.5L) + 80D (₹25K)₹12,75,000₹2,02,800
+ Home loan interest (₹2L) on top of the above₹10,75,000₹1,40,400

Even in the most favourable Old Regime scenario — full 80C, full 80D, and ₹2 lakh of home loan interest — the New Regime's ₹97,500 still wins by ₹42,900. You would need deductions well beyond these three combined (for example, HRA on top of a large home loan) to make the Old Regime competitive at this income level.

Pro tip: Don't estimate this by hand for your actual numbers — other income (rental, interest, capital gains), employer NPS contributions, and HRA all shift the answer. Run your exact figures through the Income Tax Calculator, which compares both regimes side by side.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much tax do I pay on a ₹15 lakh salary under the New Regime?
₹97,500, assuming ₹15 lakh is your full gross salary with no other income. That's after the ₹75,000 standard deduction brings taxable income to ₹14,25,000, taxed across the FY 2025-26 New Regime slabs plus 4% cess.
How much tax do I pay on a ₹15 lakh salary under the Old Regime?
₹2,57,400 with no deductions beyond the ₹50,000 standard deduction. That drops to ₹2,02,800 with Section 80C (₹1.5 lakh) and 80D (₹25,000) claimed, or ₹1,40,400 if you also have ₹2 lakh of home loan interest to deduct.
Is the New Regime always better at ₹15 lakh income?
In almost every realistic case, yes. Even stacking 80C, 80D, and ₹2 lakh of home loan interest under the Old Regime (₹1,40,400) still costs more than the New Regime's flat ₹97,500.
What would make the Old Regime worth choosing at this income?
A combination of large deductions — full 80C, 80D, home loan interest, and HRA together — could close the gap, but each of those needs to actually apply to your situation. Run your specific numbers through the calculator rather than assuming.
Does this calculation include other income like rent or interest?
No — this example assumes ₹15 lakh is entirely salary income with no other sources. Rental income, interest income, or capital gains would change the taxable income and the comparison.
