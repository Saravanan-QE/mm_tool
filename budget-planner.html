Budget Summary

How Budget Planning Works

A monthly budget helps you see exactly where your money goes. By planning ahead, you can:

  • Track all your expenses in one place
  • Identify areas where you're overspending
  • Build an emergency fund
  • Work toward savings goals
  • Reduce financial stress

Budget Categories Explained

Housing & Rent: Your largest expense. Should be 25-30% of income.
Food & Groceries: Typically 10-15% of income. Cook at home to save.
Utilities: Essential services. Usually 5-10% of income.
Transportation: Getting around. Aim for 10-15% of income.
Entertainment: Fun and hobbies. Keep to 5-10% of income.
Insurance: Protection. Budget 3-5% for necessary coverage.
Savings: Your financial future. Aim for 20% of income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I create a realistic budget?
Review your actual spending for the past 3 months. Use that data as your baseline. Then adjust based on upcoming changes like new expenses or income changes.
What's the 50/30/20 budget rule?
50% for needs (rent, food, utilities), 30% for wants (entertainment, dining), 20% for savings and debt. Use this as a guideline, not a strict rule.
How often should I review my budget?
Monthly. Spend 15 minutes each month reviewing actuals vs. planned. Adjust categories based on real spending.
What if I don't have ₹20% to save?
Start with whatever you can save, even ₹500/month. As you reduce expenses, increase your savings rate. Every rupee saved helps.
How do I find extra money to save?
Track spending, negotiate bills, use public transport, cook at home, cut subscriptions you don't use. Small changes add up.
Should my budget include debt payments?
Yes. Include loan EMI in your budget. You can track it separately or include it in the relevant category.
