How Gold Investment Returns Work

Gold investment returns depend on the price appreciation of gold over your holding period. The absolute return tells you the total percentage gain, while CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) smooths this into an annualised figure for easy comparison with other investments.

CAGR = ((Current Value / Investment)1/n - 1) × 100

  • Physical Gold — returns are reduced by making charges (8-25%) which are not recovered on resale.
  • Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) — earns 2.5% p.a. interest on top of gold price gains. Capital gains are tax-free if held to maturity (8 years).
  • Gold ETF — tracks gold price with 0.5-1% annual expense ratio deducted from NAV.
  • Digital Gold — buy/sell spread of 2-3% acts as an implicit cost.

Example: ₹1,00,000 invested at ₹7,800/g buys 12.82g. If gold reaches ₹8,500/g, the value is ₹1,08,974 — a CAGR of ~1.74% over 5 years. An SGB would add ₹12,500 in interest, making the total ₹1,21,474.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is gold a good investment in India?
Gold is a proven hedge against inflation and currency depreciation. Financial advisors recommend allocating 5-15% of your portfolio to gold for diversification. Over the past 20 years, gold has delivered ~11% CAGR in INR terms.
SGB vs Physical Gold — which is better?
SGB offers 2.5% annual interest on top of gold price appreciation, and LTCG is tax-free if held to maturity (8 years). Physical gold has making charges (8-25%) and storage costs, making SGB the better financial investment in most cases.
How is gold taxed in India?
Physical and digital gold: 12.5% LTCG after 24 months of holding. Sovereign Gold Bonds: capital gains are completely tax-free if held to maturity (8 years); interest is taxable at slab rate. Gold ETF: 12.5% LTCG after 12 months.
What is the current gold price in India?
Gold prices change daily based on international spot prices and USD/INR exchange rate. Check live prices on MCX or IBJA (Indian Bullion and Jewellers Association). This calculator uses the price you enter manually.
Can I buy Sovereign Gold Bonds anytime?
SGBs are issued in tranches by RBI, typically 4-6 times a year. You can also buy previously issued SGBs on NSE/BSE at the prevailing market price through your demat account.
Gold ETF vs Gold Mutual Fund — what is the difference?
Gold ETF requires a demat account and trades on stock exchanges like shares. Gold mutual funds invest in Gold ETFs and don't need a demat account. Both track the gold price with a 0.5-1% annual expense ratio.
